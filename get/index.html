Model & calculation details

A binomial logistic regression is fitted by maximum likelihood to one response per intermediate card. The two reference cards are never included as observations.

p(full glare | x) = 1 / [1 + exp(−(β₀ + β₁x))]
PSE = −β₀ / β₁

Response coding. No effect = 0; full glare = 1. When you enter the no-effect cards, the app assigns 1 to the remaining cards. The PSE is the 50% category boundary, without a 0.5 guessing floor.

Scale and slope. x is the actual card value specified above; values are not automatically normalized. The displayed slope is the signed logit coefficient β₁, not the probability derivative (which is β₁/4 at the PSE). Consecutive numbering of a shorter deck changes the scale of both PSE and slope.

Hosmer–Lemeshow. Predicted probabilities are divided by quantile cut points (R type 7, right-closed intervals; tied cut points are merged). Observed and expected counts for both categories give χ², with G − 2 degrees of freedom for G nonempty groups. This follows the grouping convention of ResourceSelection::hoslem.test. The default is 10 requested groups. A small p-value indicates evidence of lack of fit; a large p-value does not establish a good fit. Sparse expected counts can make this approximation unreliable.

Estimation limits. A single response category or complete separation has no finite ordinary maximum-likelihood solution. A flat function has no identifiable PSE. Negative slopes and PSEs outside the tested range are flagged. The app does not add artificial trials, penalize coefficients or clamp the PSE to the card range.

Relation to the papers. Facchin et al. (2017) describe 99 classified cards with reference labels 0 and 101. Maddaluno et al. (2019) use 49 intermediate cards and explicitly specify a logit model and the Hosmer–Lemeshow test. Neither article lists the exact 49-card subset or specifies the number of goodness-of-fit groups. This implementation makes those choices explicit. The negative mean slopes printed in Facchin et al. are not imposed: the sign here follows the stated coding, 1 = full glare, against increasing card values.
